01 / PROJECT OVERVIEW

Connecting pipeline tools to musical questions.

The project asks how musical features and artist characteristics are associated with track popularity.

Developed for DS 8003 — Management of Big Data and Tools at Toronto Metropolitan University, the group workflow used Apache NiFi for ingestion and initial preparation, Hadoop HDFS for storage, Apache Spark for analytical processing, and Tableau for visual interpretation.

The project report describes ingestion of a 114,000+ track dataset. A separate, supplied analytical file contains 2,400 rows and 2,302 distinct track IDs. The release-year analyses cover 2000–2023; these files should not be treated as one continuous sample.

02 / ANALYTICAL QUESTIONS

Five questions about track popularity.

Each investigation produces a reusable CSV summary for comparing groups across release years.

01

How does popularity vary across genres?

Normalize genre labels and compare yearly average popularity and the share of tracks scoring at least 80.

02

How do short, medium, and long tracks compare?

Group track durations into less than 3 minutes, 3–5 minutes, and more than 5 minutes.

03

How closely are danceability and popularity related?

Calculate yearly averages and Pearson correlations to examine the strength and direction of the relationship.

04

Which tempo zones have higher popularity?

Compare slow, medium, and fast tracks within each release year.

05

How does artist popularity relate to track popularity?

Compare track scores and top-track shares across artist popularity bands.

06 / ANALYTICAL RESULTS

Reading each result in context.

These portfolio charts were recreated from the repository’s exported PySpark CSVs. They are static views, rather than the original Tableau workbook. Click a chart to inspect it at full size.

GENRE POPULARITY

Pop and rap remain visible across the sample.

The genre job splits multi-value artist genre labels, standardizes their spelling, removes duplicate track–genre pairs, and groups results by release year. The script retains groups with at least 10 tracks.

In the 2023 export, rap averaged 75.6 popularity across 12 tracks and pop averaged 74.5 across 22 tracks. Only these two labels are shown here; missing year–genre groups are left as gaps.

DURATION & POPULARITY

Short tracks led the 2023 duration comparison.

In 2023, the short-track group averaged 85.1 popularity, compared with 75.4 for medium tracks and 78.5 for long tracks. The groups contained 15, 39, and 2 records respectively.

The chart shows variation across years rather than a universal advantage for short songs. The supplied duration_ms field contains minute values; the analysis uses boundaries of 3 and 5 minutes.

View source results ↗

DANCEABILITY

A weak relationship across individual years.

All 24 yearly Pearson correlations between danceability and track popularity have an absolute value below 0.30. The exported values range from approximately −0.205 to +0.272.

This result gives limited support to danceability as a standalone explanation for popularity in this sample. Correlation does not measure the effects of other musical or artist characteristics.

View source results ↗

TEMPO ZONES

Medium tempo performed strongly in the 2023 export.

The 100–130 BPM group averaged 81.8 popularity in 2023, compared with 77.3 for slower tracks and 74.9 for faster tracks. Sixteen of its 20 records met the popularity threshold of 80.

The analysis compares tempo groups within release years. It does not establish that changing a song’s tempo would increase its popularity.

View source results ↗

ARTIST POPULARITY

Artist bands show differences, with exceptions.

The 2023 export shows average track popularity of 81.2 for the artist-popularity band 80–89, versus 73.6 for 70–79 and 73.1 for 90–99. The score-100 group averaged 86.0 but contains only two records.

The relationship is not strictly increasing across bands. Counts are shown so that small groups remain visible. The script’s exported label 100–109 is displayed as score 100, consistent with its score cap.

07 / CONTRIBUTION & REPRODUCIBILITY

Hands-on pipeline work, with a clear record of scope.

My main contribution to this group project focused on the Apache NiFi and Hadoop HDFS ingestion and storage workflow. The repository also captures the PySpark investigations, exported results, and Tableau-based interpretation developed for the project.

Reproducing the complete workflow requires more than the repository alone: the NiFi flow definition and Tableau workbook are absent, HDFS paths are hardcoded, and Spark/Python versions are unspecified. The difference between the 2,400 supplied rows and the 1,553-record exports also needs a documented explanation.

The duration field is named duration_ms but contains minute values, including one zero. Its script and export use different aggregation aliases. The artist-band label for score 100 also needs correction in the script. These details matter when extending or rerunning the analysis.

Popularity scores are time-sensitive, the sampling method is incompletely documented, and some groups are small. The results show associations in the exports and do not establish the causes of popularity, streaming trends, or commercial success.

08 / PROJECT TAKEAWAY

Connecting data infrastructure to interpretable analysis.

This project demonstrates how ingestion, distributed storage, analytical processing, and visualization fit together. It strengthened my experience with Apache NiFi and HDFS while connecting infrastructure work to questions that people can explore through data.

The next iteration would parameterize the jobs, validate input schemas and units, reconcile the analytical datasets, and package the missing flow and visualization files for a reproducible run.
