01 / PROJECT OVERVIEW

Understanding rental conditions beyond a single price.

The highest-rent market is not always the market with the strongest rent growth or the tightest availability.

This project is framed around the needs of an Ontario Housing Strategy & Market Intelligence Team. It brings rent, vacancy, rental stock, turnover, and family income into one analytical view to support descriptive market comparisons.

I combined CMHC Rental Market Reports for 2019–2025 with Statistics Canada Table 11-10-0017-01 income observations for 2019–2023. The final report covers 15 Ontario markets and five apartment unit categories, from studio units to the published Total category.

From public source files to reusable market insights.

Each tool serves a distinct purpose, with SQL owning the analytical calculations.

DATASET

CMHC Rental Reports & Statistics Canada Income Data

Rental observations from seven annual CMHC editions were aligned with five years of median after-tax family income data. Geographic names were standardized across the sources before building market-year analytical views.

05 / AFFORDABILITY & RENTAL PRESSURE

Connecting rental costs to local income and availability.

The rent-to-income indicator adds economic context to market comparisons, while vacancy and rent growth help describe local rental pressure.

Toronto’s indicator was 36.2% in 2023.

Toronto had the highest rent-to-income indicator among the 15 markets in 2023.

The calculation annualizes average monthly rent and divides it by median after-tax family income. For Toronto, an average rent of $1,826 per month and income of $60,510 produced an indicator of approximately 36.2%.

These are aggregate market statistics that may describe different households. The result is useful for comparing markets, but it is not the actual housing-cost burden of individual renters. Income-based comparisons end in 2023.

ANALYTICAL TAKEAWAY

Consider rent levels, growth, vacancy, and income together. Stronger rent growth alongside lower vacancy can indicate rental pressure, but the dashboard describes patterns rather than demonstrating a causal relationship.

07 / PROJECT TAKEAWAY & METHODOLOGY

Useful comparisons start with careful definitions.

This project demonstrates source auditing, reproducible extraction, relational data modeling, time-based SQL calculations, and dashboard communication. Together, the outputs support comparisons of rent levels, growth, availability, and local income without reducing rental pressure to a single number.

CMHC figures cover the private apartment rental market in its reports. The Total category is not added to its component unit categories. Unavailable observations, including source symbols such as ** and ++, remain missing rather than estimated.

Calculated rent growth is distinct from CMHC’s published rent-change measure. The rent-to-income indicator combines aggregate rent and family income statistics that may describe different households; it is not an official household affordability rate. Income values are not extended beyond 2023.

Relationships between vacancy and rent growth are descriptive and do not establish causation. The report provides context for market comparison rather than a prediction of future rents.
